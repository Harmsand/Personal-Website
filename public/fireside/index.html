    Psychological safety: making it normal to speak up

    Dr. Maya Okafor, live keynote

    Written around your goals: leading your first team well, and staying steady under pressure.

    Three ideas for a new team lead

    1. Ask for bad news before good news. Starting a check-in with “what’s not working?” tells the team problems are welcome.You said you're about to lead a team. This sets the tone in week one.
    2. Thank the messenger, every time. The first person to raise a problem watches how you react, and so does everyone else.
    3. Under pressure, slow your first reply. A calm “tell me more” buys time and keeps people talking.Ties to your goal of staying steady under pressure.

    Try this week

    • Open your next team meeting with one question: “What’s one thing slowing you down?”
    • When someone flags a mistake, say thank you before you say anything else.
    • Notice one moment you felt rushed to answer. Write down what you'd say with ten more seconds.

    A question to sit with

    When did someone last bring you a problem early, and what did you do that made it easy for them?
